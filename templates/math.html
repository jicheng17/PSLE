Convert first: units geography questions love to mix

Length1 m = 100 cm  ·  1 km = 1 000 m  ·  1 km = 100 000 cm
Area1 m² = 10 000 cm²  ·  1 ha = 10 000 m² extra  ·  1 km² = 1 000 000 m²
Volume1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³  ·  1 L = 1 000 mL = 1 000 cm³  ·  1 m³ = 1 000 L
Time1 h = 60 min  ·  15 min = 0.25 h  ·  30 min = 0.5 h  ·  45 min = 0.75 h
Rain1 mm of rain on 1 m² = 1 L of water

Maps and scale

Ratio

Key rules

  • Scale 1 : 50 0001 cm on the map is 50 000 cm in real life, which is 500 m.
  • Actual = map distance × scale numberGoing from the map to real life, multiply.
  • Map = actual ÷ scale numberGoing from real life to the map, divide.
  • 1 : 100 000 means 1 cm = 1 kmHandy shortcut for road maps.
  • "1 cm to 2 km" is a scale tooSame idea, just already in words. Change to one unit first.

Worked example: map to real life

A map has a scale of 1 : 20 000. Two MRT stations are 6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in km?

  • 6 × 20 000 = 120 000 cm
  • 120 000 ÷ 100 = 1 200 m
  • 1 200 ÷ 1 000 = 1.2 km
1.2 km

Worked example: real life to map

A park is 900 m long. How long is it on a map with scale 1 : 30 000?

  • 900 m = 90 000 cm
  • 90 000 ÷ 30 000 = 3
3 cm
Watch out: convert to centimetres before you multiply or divide. A bigger scale number means a smaller picture. Beyond PSLE: areas use the scale squared, so on a 1 : 10 000 map, 1 cm² stands for 1 ha.
Your turn

On a 1 : 25 000 map, two bus stops are 8 cm apart. How far is that in km?

8 × 25 000 = 200 000 cm = 2 000 m = 2 km

Journeys: distance, speed, time

Rate and speed

Key rules

  • Distance = Speed × TimeThe base formula. Cover the one you want in the triangle D / (S × T).
  • Speed = Distance ÷ TimeUnits: km/h needs km and hours.
  • Time = Distance ÷ SpeedTurn 0.5 h back into 30 min at the end.
  • Average speed = total distance ÷ total timeNever the average of two speeds.
  • Towards each other: add speeds. Same direction: subtract.Two towns 300 km apart, cars at 50 and 70 km/h: 300 ÷ 120 = 2.5 h to meet.
  • Time on a 24-hour clock1300 is 1 pm. From 0800 add 5 h to get 1300.

Worked example: arrival time

A coach leaves Singapore at 0800 for Kuala Lumpur, about 350 km away, at an average speed of 70 km/h. When does it arrive?

  • Time = 350 ÷ 70 = 5 h
  • 0800 + 5 h = 1300
1300 (1 pm)

Worked example: average speed

A car travels 60 km at 40 km/h, then another 60 km at 60 km/h. What is its average speed for the whole trip?

  • Time 1: 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 h
  • Time 2: 60 ÷ 60 = 1 h
  • Total: 120 km in 2.5 h
  • 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48
48 km/h
Watch out: 50 km/h is the tempting answer above, and it is wrong. The car spends longer at the slower speed, so the average is pulled below 50.
Your turn

A boat sails 45 km at 30 km/h and leaves at 0930. When does it arrive?

45 ÷ 30 = 1.5 h = 1 h 30 min. 0930 + 1 h 30 min = 1100

Weather data: graphs and averages

Statistics

Key rules

  • Average = total ÷ number of itemsRainfall, temperature, population per year.
  • Total = average × number of itemsThe trick for missing-value questions.
  • Pie chart angle = share × 360°Percentage = angle ÷ 360 × 100.
  • % change = change ÷ original × 100%Divide by the starting amount, not the new one.

Reading a graph

  • 1. Read the title and both axis labels, with units.
  • 2. Work out what one small square is worth.
  • 3. Check whether the axis starts at 0.
  • 4. Use a ruler to trace to the axis.

Worked example: daily rainfall

Rainfall (mm) from Monday to Friday is shown. What is the average daily rainfall, and how much more rain fell on the wettest day than the driest?

  • Total = 12 + 0 + 8 + 20 + 10 = 50 mm
  • Average = 50 ÷ 5 = 10 mm
  • Wettest (Thu) 20 − driest (Tue) 0 = 20 mm
10 mm a day; 20 mm more

Example numbers made up for practice.

Two quick ones

A pie chart shows 108° for housing. What percentage of the land is housing?

  • 108 ÷ 360 × 100 = 30
30%

A population grows from 4 million to 5 million. What is the percentage increase?

  • (5 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 = 25
25%
Watch out: percentage change always divides by the original. Going 4 to 5 is up 25%, but going 5 back to 4 is down only 20%.
Your turn

The average rainfall for 4 months is 180 mm. The 5th month has 230 mm. What is the average for all 5 months?

Total = 4 × 180 + 230 = 950. 950 ÷ 5 = 190 mm

Land and water: area, volume, ratio

Measurement

Key rules

  • Rectangle area = length × breadthReservoirs, fields, rooftops.
  • Triangle area = ½ × base × heightHeight is straight up, at a right angle to the base.
  • Circle area = π × r × rUse 3.14 or 22/7 as the question says. Radius is half the diameter.
  • Cuboid volume = l × b × hWater in a tank or reservoir.
  • Rain volume = area × depth of rainPut the depth in the same unit as the area first, so 30 mm = 0.03 m for an area in m².
  • Ratio sharing: total ÷ total parts = 1 partThen multiply by each share.

Worked example: rain on a roof

A rectangular roof is 20 m by 10 m. During a storm, 30 mm of rain falls. How many litres of rainwater land on the roof?

  • Area = 20 × 10 = 200 m²
  • 30 mm = 0.03 m
  • Volume = 200 × 0.03 = 6 m³
  • 6 × 1 000 = 6 000 L
6 000 L

Shortcut: 1 mm on 1 m² is 1 L, so 200 × 30 = 6 000 L.

Worked example: sharing land by ratio

An island has an area of 720 km². The ratio of built-up land to green land to water is 5 : 3 : 1. What is the area of green land?

  • Total parts = 5 + 3 + 1 = 9
  • 1 part = 720 ÷ 9 = 80 km²
  • Green = 3 × 80 = 240 km²
240 km²
Watch out: mixing mm with m is the classic slip. Change everything to one unit before multiplying, and write the unit in your answer.
Your turn

A reservoir surface is 400 m long and 250 m wide. 20 mm of rain falls on it. Find the volume of rainwater in m³ and in litres.

400 × 250 × 0.02 = 2 000 m³ = 2 000 000 L

Compass turns

Angles

Key rules

  • 8 points, each 45° apartMeasured clockwise from North (N = 0°, E = 90°, S = 180°, W = 270°).
  • Full turn = 360°, half turn = 180°, quarter turn = 90°A half turn always gives the opposite direction.
  • Clockwise adds. Anticlockwise subtracts.If you pass 360°, subtract 360 to come back around.

Worked example: turn and read the new direction

A hiker faces North-East and turns 135° clockwise. Which direction is the hiker facing now?

  • North-East = 45°
  • 45° + 135° = 180°
  • 180° is due South
South

Worked example: turning past North

A ship faces South-West and makes a half turn. Which way does it face?

  • South-West = 225°
  • 225° + 180° = 405°, and 405° − 360° = 45°
North-East
Watch out: read the turn direction in the question. A clockwise turn moves N → E → S → W; anticlockwise goes the other way round.
Your turn

A pilot faces West and turns 135° clockwise. Which direction now?

West = 270°. 270° + 135° = 405°, minus 360° = 45°, so North-East

Exam habits for these questions

  • Underline the scale and unitsCircle km, m, cm, mm, h and min in the question.
  • Convert before you calculateOne unit throughout, then convert the final answer.
  • Sketch itA quick bar model or route line shows what is being asked.
  • Show each stepMethod marks come from working, not just the answer.
  • Write the unit1.2 km, 6 000 L, 48 km/h. No unit, no mark.
  • Sense-checkIs a 1.2 km walk between stations sensible? Is 6 000 L on a roof sensible?

Angles

Geometry

Angle facts at a glance

Key rules

  • Angles on a straight line = 180°A half turn.
  • Angles at a point = 360°A full turn.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equalWhere two straight lines cross.
  • Angles in a triangle = 180°Every triangle, any shape.
  • Isosceles: base angles equal. Equilateral: all 60°.Mark the equal sides first, then the equal angles.
  • Parallelogram, rhombus: opposite angles equal; neighbours add to 180°Learn these — questions won't remind you.

Worked example: angles at a point

Three angles meet at a point: 130°, 95° and x. Find x.

  • x = 360° − 130° − 95°
  • = 135°
135°

Worked example: isosceles triangle

An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 52°. Find each base angle.

  • Two base angles = 180° − 52° = 128°
  • One base angle = 128° ÷ 2 = 64°
64°

Worked example: parallelogram

In parallelogram PQRS, angle P is 72°. Find angle Q.

  • P and Q are neighbours (they share a side)
  • Q = 180° − 72° = 108°
108°
Watch out: the question usually won't tell you a triangle is isosceles or a shape is a rhombus — you're expected to spot it from tick marks or the name given. Learn the shape properties by heart.
Your turn

Angles a, 50° and 75° sit together on a straight line. Find a.

a = 180° − 50° − 75° = 55°

Area of triangles

Measurement

The height is always perpendicular to the base

Key rules

  • Area of triangle = ½ × base × heightA triangle is always half of the rectangle with the same base and height.
  • Base and height must meet at a right angleThe height is never a slanted side.
  • Working backwards: base = 2 × area ÷ heightUndo the ½ by doubling first.
  • Composite figure: split it, or complete it then subtractBreak the shape into rectangles and triangles you already know how to find.

Worked example: area of a triangle

A triangle has a base of 14 cm and a height of 9 cm. Find its area.

  • ½ × 14 × 9 = 63
63 cm²

Worked example: composite figure

A figure is made of a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a right triangle of base 10 cm and height 4 cm attached to one side. Find the total area.

  • Rectangle = 10 × 6 = 60 cm²
  • Triangle = ½ × 10 × 4 = 20 cm²
  • 60 + 20 = 80
80 cm²

Worked example: missing height

A triangle has an area of 36 cm² and a base of 8 cm. Find its height.

  • Height = 2 × 36 ÷ 8
  • = 72 ÷ 8 = 9
9 cm
Watch out: never use a slanted side as the height — look for the small square that marks the right angle, or check the height is drawn straight up from the base (even if that means it falls outside the triangle).
Your turn

A triangle has an area of 45 cm² and a height of 10 cm. Find its base.

Base = 2 × 45 ÷ 10 = 90 ÷ 10 = 9 cm

Volume of cubes and cuboids

Measurement

A box packed with unit cubes

Key rules

  • Cuboid: V = l × b × hLength × breadth × height.
  • Cube: V = s × s × sAll edges are equal.
  • 1 L = 1 000 mL = 1 000 cm³Divide cm³ by 1 000 to get litres.
  • Missing edge: h = V ÷ (l × b)Divide the volume by the two edges you know.
  • Number of small cubes = big volume ÷ small volumeOr count how many fit along each edge and multiply.

Worked example: finding volume

A box is 12 cm long, 8 cm wide and 5 cm tall. Find its volume.

  • V = 12 × 8 × 5 = 480
480 cm³

Worked example: missing height

A cuboid has a volume of 252 cm³. Its length is 9 cm and its breadth is 7 cm. Find its height.

  • h = 252 ÷ (9 × 7)
  • = 252 ÷ 63 = 4
4 cm

Worked example: packing small cubes

A large box measures 40 cm by 30 cm by 20 cm. How many small cubes of edge 10 cm can exactly fill it?

  • Large box volume = 40 × 30 × 20 = 24 000 cm³
  • Small cube volume = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1 000 cm³
  • 24 000 ÷ 1 000 = 24
24 small cubes
Watch out: volume is measured in cubic units (cm³), never plain cm. When counting how many small cubes fit, make sure the small cube's edge divides exactly into each of the box's three edges — draw it out if you're not sure.
Your turn

A cube has edges of 6 cm. Find its volume, and say how many mL of water it can hold.

6 × 6 × 6 = 216 cm³ = 216 mL

Fractions

Number

"Of" a quantity means multiply

Key rules

  • Adding or subtracting: use a common denominator firstConvert both fractions before you add or subtract.
  • Mixed numbers: add/subtract whole parts and fraction partsRegroup if the fraction part doesn't have enough to subtract.
  • Fraction × whole number: multiply the numerator only3/5 × 20 = (3 × 20) ÷ 5
  • Fraction × fraction: multiply straight acrossNumerator × numerator, denominator × denominator, then simplify.
  • Mixed number × whole number: convert to an improper fraction firstThen multiply as usual.

Worked example: adding mixed numbers

Find 2 1/4 + 1 5/6.

  • Common denominator is 12: 2 3/12 + 1 10/12
  • = 3 13/12
  • = 4 1/12
4 1/12

Worked example: fraction of a quantity

There are 20 sweets in a bag. 3/5 of them are red. How many are red?

  • 3/5 × 20 = (3 × 20) ÷ 5
  • = 60 ÷ 5 = 12
12 sweets

Worked example: multiplying two fractions

Find 2/3 × 3/8.

  • (2 × 3) ÷ (3 × 8) = 6/24
  • = 1/4
1/4
Watch out: you can only add or subtract fractions once they share a denominator — never add numerators and denominators separately. When multiplying, look for numbers that cancel diagonally first; it keeps the numbers small.
Your turn

Find 3/4 × 2/9.

(3 × 2) ÷ (4 × 9) = 6/36 = 1/6

Decimals

Number

Key rules

  • × 10, 100, 1 000: move the point right1, 2 or 3 places. 0.6 × 100 = 60
  • ÷ 10, 100, 1 000: move the point left4.5 ÷ 100 = 0.045
  • Multiplying by a whole number: multiply normally, then place the pointCount the decimal places in the number you started with.
  • Dividing by a whole number: divide normally, point stays lined upSame place value, straight down.
  • Money: 2 decimal placesThe nearest cent.

Worked example: multiplying decimals

A pencil costs $0.65. Find the cost of 6 pencils.

  • 0.65 × 6 = 3.90
$3.90

Worked example: dividing decimals

A ribbon 9.6 m long is cut equally into 4 pieces. Find the length of each piece.

  • 9.6 ÷ 4 = 2.4
2.4 m

Worked example: × 100 shortcut

A jug holds 1.25 L of juice. Find the total volume of 100 identical jugs, in litres.

  • 1.25 × 100 = 125
125 L
Watch out: "0.6" and "0.06" are not the same size — line up the decimal point carefully when you write your working. When multiplying two decimals, count the total decimal places in both numbers; when multiplying a decimal by a whole number, only the decimal counts.
Your turn

Apple juice costs $1.85 per bottle. Find the cost of 8 bottles.

1.85 × 8 = $14.80

Rate

Number

Key rules

  • Rate = total ÷ time (or ÷ quantity)An amount for every one unit — L/min, $/kg, pages/min.
  • Total = rate × timeMultiply the rate by however many units you have.
  • Time = total ÷ rateUndo the multiplication.
  • Better buy: compare the cost for the same unitFind the cost per kg (or per item) for each option.

Worked example: basic rate

A tap fills a tank at a rate of 8 litres per minute. How many litres flow in 15 minutes?

  • 8 × 15 = 120
120 L

Worked example: finding the rate, then the time

A printer prints 45 pages in 5 minutes. Find its rate, then find how long it takes to print 180 pages.

  • Rate = 45 ÷ 5 = 9 pages per minute
  • Time = 180 ÷ 9 = 20
9 pages/min; 20 minutes

Worked example: better buy

A 2 kg bag of rice costs $5.60. A 5 kg bag costs $13.50. Which is the better buy?

  • 2 kg bag: 5.60 ÷ 2 = $2.80 per kg
  • 5 kg bag: 13.50 ÷ 5 = $2.70 per kg
The 5 kg bag
Watch out: identify what one "unit" of the rate actually is — per minute, per kg, per item — before you multiply or divide. Keep your answer's unit the same as the rate's unit.
Your turn

A machine packs 240 boxes in 6 hours. Find its rate, then find how long it takes to pack 600 boxes.

Rate = 240 ÷ 6 = 40 boxes/hour. Time = 600 ÷ 40 = 15 hours

Percentage

Number

Key rules

  • Part = percentage/100 × wholeFinding a percentage of a quantity.
  • Percentage = part/whole × 100%Expressing one quantity as a percentage of another.
  • Whole = part ÷ percentage × 100Working backwards from a part to the whole.
  • 100% is always the whole thingBe sure what the question calls "the whole" before you start.

Worked example: percentage of a quantity

Find 40% of 350.

  • 40/100 × 350 = 140
140

Worked example: expressing as a percentage

Ravi answered 18 out of 24 questions correctly. What percentage is that?

  • 18/24 × 100% = 75%
75%

Worked example: finding the whole

45 is 30% of a number. Find the number.

  • Whole = 45 ÷ 30 × 100
  • = 150
150
Watch out: "part/whole × 100%" and "percentage/100 × whole" are the same rule read in opposite directions — write down which one the question is asking for before calculating, not after.
Your turn

36 is 20% of a number. Find the number.

36 ÷ 20 × 100 = 180

Exam habits for P5 topics

  • Mark the figureFor angles, write every angle you find straight onto the diagram, with the reason beside it.
  • Name the wholeFor percentage and rate questions, be sure what "the whole" or "one unit" refers to.
  • Keep units consistentcm³ to litres, minutes to hours — convert before you combine numbers.
  • Line up decimal pointsFor addition and subtraction; for multiplication, count decimal places instead.
  • Show every stepMethod marks are given even if the final answer slips.
