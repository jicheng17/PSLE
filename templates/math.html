Convert first: units geography questions love to mix

Length1 m = 100 cm  ·  1 km = 1 000 m  ·  1 km = 100 000 cm
Area1 m² = 10 000 cm²  ·  1 ha = 10 000 m² extra  ·  1 km² = 1 000 000 m²
Volume1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³  ·  1 L = 1 000 mL = 1 000 cm³  ·  1 m³ = 1 000 L
Time1 h = 60 min  ·  15 min = 0.25 h  ·  30 min = 0.5 h  ·  45 min = 0.75 h
Rain1 mm of rain on 1 m² = 1 L of water

Maps and scale

Ratio

Key rules

  • Scale 1 : 50 0001 cm on the map is 50 000 cm in real life, which is 500 m.
  • Actual = map distance × scale numberGoing from the map to real life, multiply.
  • Map = actual ÷ scale numberGoing from real life to the map, divide.
  • 1 : 100 000 means 1 cm = 1 kmHandy shortcut for road maps.
  • "1 cm to 2 km" is a scale tooSame idea, just already in words. Change to one unit first.

Worked example: map to real life

A map has a scale of 1 : 20 000. Two MRT stations are 6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they in km?

  • 6 × 20 000 = 120 000 cm
  • 120 000 ÷ 100 = 1 200 m
  • 1 200 ÷ 1 000 = 1.2 km
1.2 km

Worked example: real life to map

A park is 900 m long. How long is it on a map with scale 1 : 30 000?

  • 900 m = 90 000 cm
  • 90 000 ÷ 30 000 = 3
3 cm
Watch out: convert to centimetres before you multiply or divide. A bigger scale number means a smaller picture. Beyond PSLE: areas use the scale squared, so on a 1 : 10 000 map, 1 cm² stands for 1 ha.
Your turn

On a 1 : 25 000 map, two bus stops are 8 cm apart. How far is that in km?

8 × 25 000 = 200 000 cm = 2 000 m = 2 km

Journeys: distance, speed, time

Rate and speed

Key rules

  • Distance = Speed × TimeThe base formula. Cover the one you want in the triangle D / (S × T).
  • Speed = Distance ÷ TimeUnits: km/h needs km and hours.
  • Time = Distance ÷ SpeedTurn 0.5 h back into 30 min at the end.
  • Average speed = total distance ÷ total timeNever the average of two speeds.
  • Towards each other: add speeds. Same direction: subtract.Two towns 300 km apart, cars at 50 and 70 km/h: 300 ÷ 120 = 2.5 h to meet.
  • Time on a 24-hour clock1300 is 1 pm. From 0800 add 5 h to get 1300.

Worked example: arrival time

A coach leaves Singapore at 0800 for Kuala Lumpur, about 350 km away, at an average speed of 70 km/h. When does it arrive?

  • Time = 350 ÷ 70 = 5 h
  • 0800 + 5 h = 1300
1300 (1 pm)

Worked example: average speed

A car travels 60 km at 40 km/h, then another 60 km at 60 km/h. What is its average speed for the whole trip?

  • Time 1: 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 h
  • Time 2: 60 ÷ 60 = 1 h
  • Total: 120 km in 2.5 h
  • 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48
48 km/h
Watch out: 50 km/h is the tempting answer above, and it is wrong. The car spends longer at the slower speed, so the average is pulled below 50.
Your turn

A boat sails 45 km at 30 km/h and leaves at 0930. When does it arrive?

45 ÷ 30 = 1.5 h = 1 h 30 min. 0930 + 1 h 30 min = 1100

Weather data: graphs and averages

Statistics

Key rules

  • Average = total ÷ number of itemsRainfall, temperature, population per year.
  • Total = average × number of itemsThe trick for missing-value questions.
  • Pie chart angle = share × 360°Percentage = angle ÷ 360 × 100.
  • % change = change ÷ original × 100%Divide by the starting amount, not the new one.

Reading a graph

  • 1. Read the title and both axis labels, with units.
  • 2. Work out what one small square is worth.
  • 3. Check whether the axis starts at 0.
  • 4. Use a ruler to trace to the axis.

Worked example: daily rainfall

Rainfall (mm) from Monday to Friday is shown. What is the average daily rainfall, and how much more rain fell on the wettest day than the driest?

  • Total = 12 + 0 + 8 + 20 + 10 = 50 mm
  • Average = 50 ÷ 5 = 10 mm
  • Wettest (Thu) 20 − driest (Tue) 0 = 20 mm
10 mm a day; 20 mm more

Example numbers made up for practice.

Two quick ones

A pie chart shows 108° for housing. What percentage of the land is housing?

  • 108 ÷ 360 × 100 = 30
30%

A population grows from 4 million to 5 million. What is the percentage increase?

  • (5 − 4) ÷ 4 × 100 = 25
25%
Watch out: percentage change always divides by the original. Going 4 to 5 is up 25%, but going 5 back to 4 is down only 20%.
Your turn

The average rainfall for 4 months is 180 mm. The 5th month has 230 mm. What is the average for all 5 months?

Total = 4 × 180 + 230 = 950. 950 ÷ 5 = 190 mm

Land and water: area, volume, ratio

Measurement

Key rules

  • Rectangle area = length × breadthReservoirs, fields, rooftops.
  • Triangle area = ½ × base × heightHeight is straight up, at a right angle to the base.
  • Circle area = π × r × rUse 3.14 or 22/7 as the question says. Radius is half the diameter.
  • Cuboid volume = l × b × hWater in a tank or reservoir.
  • Rain volume = area × depth of rainPut the depth in the same unit as the area first, so 30 mm = 0.03 m for an area in m².
  • Ratio sharing: total ÷ total parts = 1 partThen multiply by each share.

Worked example: rain on a roof

A rectangular roof is 20 m by 10 m. During a storm, 30 mm of rain falls. How many litres of rainwater land on the roof?

  • Area = 20 × 10 = 200 m²
  • 30 mm = 0.03 m
  • Volume = 200 × 0.03 = 6 m³
  • 6 × 1 000 = 6 000 L
6 000 L

Shortcut: 1 mm on 1 m² is 1 L, so 200 × 30 = 6 000 L.

Worked example: sharing land by ratio

An island has an area of 720 km². The ratio of built-up land to green land to water is 5 : 3 : 1. What is the area of green land?

  • Total parts = 5 + 3 + 1 = 9
  • 1 part = 720 ÷ 9 = 80 km²
  • Green = 3 × 80 = 240 km²
240 km²
Watch out: mixing mm with m is the classic slip. Change everything to one unit before multiplying, and write the unit in your answer.
Your turn

A reservoir surface is 400 m long and 250 m wide. 20 mm of rain falls on it. Find the volume of rainwater in m³ and in litres.

400 × 250 × 0.02 = 2 000 m³ = 2 000 000 L

Compass turns

Angles

Key rules

  • 8 points, each 45° apartMeasured clockwise from North (N = 0°, E = 90°, S = 180°, W = 270°).
  • Full turn = 360°, half turn = 180°, quarter turn = 90°A half turn always gives the opposite direction.
  • Clockwise adds. Anticlockwise subtracts.If you pass 360°, subtract 360 to come back around.

Worked example: turn and read the new direction

A hiker faces North-East and turns 135° clockwise. Which direction is the hiker facing now?

  • North-East = 45°
  • 45° + 135° = 180°
  • 180° is due South
South

Worked example: turning past North

A ship faces South-West and makes a half turn. Which way does it face?

  • South-West = 225°
  • 225° + 180° = 405°, and 405° − 360° = 45°
North-East
Watch out: read the turn direction in the question. A clockwise turn moves N → E → S → W; anticlockwise goes the other way round.
Your turn

A pilot faces West and turns 135° clockwise. Which direction now?

West = 270°. 270° + 135° = 405°, minus 360° = 45°, so North-East

Exam habits for these questions

  • Underline the scale and unitsCircle km, m, cm, mm, h and min in the question.
  • Convert before you calculateOne unit throughout, then convert the final answer.
  • Sketch itA quick bar model or route line shows what is being asked.
  • Show each stepMethod marks come from working, not just the answer.
  • Write the unit1.2 km, 6 000 L, 48 km/h. No unit, no mark.
  • Sense-checkIs a 1.2 km walk between stations sensible? Is 6 000 L on a roof sensible?
Contexts are Singapore-flavoured and distances are approximate. Numbers in examples are for practice, not real records.
